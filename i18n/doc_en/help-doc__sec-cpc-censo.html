CPC × Census — two complementary indicators

MAPA-GR offers two analysis pages fed by different INEP sources, each answering a different question. Understanding the distinction prevents misreadings.

In one sentence:
CPC/ENADE answers "how well does this programme educate?" — it is a quality indicator.
The Higher Education Census answers "how many are studying?" — it is a stock indicator.
AspectCPC / ENADE (the Statistics page)Census (the Higher Education Census 2017-2023 page)
What it measures Programme quality — final-year students' performance in the ENADE exam + IDD (value added) + faculty + infrastructure Student stock — how many are enrolled, how many entered, how many graduated in a year
Unit A 1 to 5 score per programme (the CPC band), or a continuous value (continuous CPC, continuous ENADE, IDD) Absolute number of students (enrolments, entrants, graduates, suspended, and so on)
Frequency Every three years, by field (the ENADE cycle) Yearly (every institution reports in October/November)
Coverage ~47 thousand programmes that took part in ENADE in the 2017, 2018, 2021, 2022 and 2023 cycles ~670 thousand rows of programme×institution×mode (≈ 9.98 million enrolments in 2023)
Useful granularity Programme by institution: comparing quality across universities Year, state, field, mode, degree type, institution, gender, race, age, funding (FIES, PROUNI), quotas
When to use it Deciding where to study, comparing quality across institutions, assessing a programme Sizing demand, analysing growth, planning education policy, checking dropout
Source INEP — Higher Education Quality Indicators INEP — Higher Education Census microdata (2017-2023)

Why not put it all on a single page?

Because quality and quantity are not comparable. A programme with CPC 5 and 50 students, and another with CPC 3 and 5,000 students describe very different realities — they do not reduce to a single metric. Each indicator contributes one dimension of Brazilian undergraduate education. Keeping the two pages apart avoids analytical confusion.

Can enrolments be estimated from the CPC?

In theory, yes: using n_inscritos (final-year students who sat ENADE) as a proxy and projecting to the total stock. We tested that formula in detail (see the metodologia/ folder in the repository). The result:

What the audit concluded: the formula enrolments ≈ n_inscritos × duration × α / r is right in the national aggregate (error ≈ ±2%), but errors by degree type, field and state reach ±150% — useless for fine-grained analysis. That is why we use the real Census, which INEP publishes yearly. The full audit and the script are documented in the metodologia/ folder.

Which page to open

  • Want to know where the best-rated programmes in your field are? → ENADE statistics
  • Want to know how many students there are and how that has grown? → Higher Education Census 2017-2023
  • Want to compare institutions and states by quality? → Main panel (CPC) or the Comparator
